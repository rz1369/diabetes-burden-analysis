01 — The Urbanization Paradox

The sign flips when you look within countries.

Across the raw country-year data, the bivariate correlation between urbanization and diabetes burden is negative: more urbanized countries tend to have lower diabetes burden, because they are also wealthier and have stronger health systems. But once country fixed effects strip out persistent between-country differences and the estimator focuses on within-country changes over time, the sign flips. As countries urbanize, their diabetes burden rises.

When a country urbanizes, its own diabetes burden rises. This is the within-country dynamic the fixed-effects estimator identifies.
Finding
The naive cross-country picture is misleading. The estimator's choice is the analysis: removing persistent cross-country differences flips the direction of the urbanization–burden relationship from negative to positive.
02 — What Drives Burden

Obesity and urbanization survive. Income and spending do not.

The preferred specification: log DALY rate as the outcome, with country and year fixed effects, country-clustered standard errors, and adult obesity controlled for (Table 5.1, column 4). Estimated on the obesity-extended panel (2000–2022, n = 4,246 across 188 countries). Of the five regressors, only two are statistically significant within countries: obesity and urbanization. The within R² is 0.134.

SOURCE: Author's regression, Table 5.1 col. 4. GBD 2023 (IHME 2025) + World Bank WDI (2026) + Human Capital Data Portal (2026).
Finding
A one-percentage-point rise in adult obesity prevalence is associated with a 0.8% rise in the diabetes DALY rate within a country (β = 0.008, p = 0.018). A one-percentage-point rise in urbanization is associated with a 0.4% rise (β = 0.004, p = 0.038). Income and health spending show no detectable within-country association over the 24-year window.
03 — The Reverse Direction

Does diabetes burden predict subsequent growth? Not at annual horizons.

The growth equation reverses the direction: does a country's lagged diabetes DALY rate carry information about its subsequent GDP per capita growth? The preferred specification (Table 5.3, column 2) is a parsimonious health-augmented Solow regression with lagged log GDP (conditional convergence), age structure, urbanization, gross fixed capital formation (Solow accumulation), and the lagged log DALY rate as the health-capital term.

Predicting annual GDP per capita growth
95% confidence intervals · two-way FE with investment control · n = 3,654 across 172 countries
SOURCE: Author's regression, Table 5.3 col. 2. GBD 2023 (IHME 2025) + World Bank WDI (2026).
Finding
Conditional convergence and capital accumulation behave exactly as the Solow framework predicts — lagged GDP at −6.56 (p < 0.001), investment rate at +0.17 (p < 0.001). The lagged log diabetes burden, however, is statistically indistinguishable from zero (β = 0.255, p = 0.815). This is a null: cross-country annual variation does not contain enough diabetes-specific signal for the within estimator to detect a growth effect at the one-year horizon.
